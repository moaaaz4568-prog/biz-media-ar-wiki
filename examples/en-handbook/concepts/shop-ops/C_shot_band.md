# C_shot_band — The 25–30 Second Shot Band

## Definition

The shot band is the pull-time window for a double espresso — 25 to 30
seconds from pump start to last drip — inside which the shop's espresso
tastes as intended. Outside the band, the same beans, dose, and machine
produce sour (fast) or bitter and muddy (slow) shots.

## Mechanism

Extraction is a race between water and coffee solids. In the band, the
water dissolves the sweet and aromatic compounds before the harsh ones
dominate. A fast pull under-extracts: the acids that need time stay
sharp, and the shot tastes sour. A slow pull over-extracts: bitterness
and sediment take over, and the shot tastes muddy. The band is therefore
a taste boundary expressed as a time, which is what makes it measurable
every single morning rather than a matter of opinion.

## Limits — when it breaks

- When grinder blades are worn, the clock reads slow for a mechanical
  reason no setting fixes — the band then measures the machine, not the
  coffee.
- When humidity rises, beans absorb moisture and the same setting pulls
  slower; the morning band from a dry week is wrong on a rainy day.
- When the machine was turned on late, warm-up sourness mimics a fast-
  pull sour taste and misleads the dial.

## Conditions

- Does the handbook's band apply to a double espresso? (Single shots
  are not covered by the source.)
- Was the machine warmed up for the full forty minutes before dial-in?
