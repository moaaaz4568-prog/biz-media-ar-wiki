# Operations Handbook — Riverside Coffee Co. (Shift Lead Edition)

This handbook is the single source of truth for shift leads at Riverside
Coffee Co. It was written by the operations team after the 2025 store
refit and reflects the way the shop actually runs, not the way the old
manual imagined it. When a rule here conflicts with habit, the handbook
wins; when reality conflicts with the handbook, file a change request and
the ops team will decide.

## Section 1: Opening the store

The opening shift starts at 6:00 sharp. The first hour decides the whole
day because morning commuters judge the shop by their first visit. Pull
the previous day's cash report before anything else, then unlock the
front door at 6:30 — never earlier, because the alarm company's grace
period ends at 6:25 and an early door means a false dispatch fee.

Espresso machines need forty minutes of warm-up time before they pull a
shot worth serving. Turn them on the moment you walk in, then do the
cash count while they heat. A machine turned on at 6:15 produces sour
shots until 6:55, and a sour first shot costs you the regular who always
orders a flat white at 6:45.

Milk arrives from the dairy at 5:50 in the back alley. Check the
temperature of every crate on arrival; anything above 4 degrees Celsius
goes back on the truck, no exceptions. In August 2025 we lost two crates
to a broken fridge van and served nothing but black coffee until noon —
that day is the reason this rule exists.

## Section 2: The espresso dial

The shot clock is the heart of quality control. A double espresso should
pull between 25 and 30 seconds; shorter tastes sour, longer tastes
bitter and muddy. Adjust the grind, not the dose, when the time drifts —
changing the dose to fix timing hides the real problem and teaches new
baristas the wrong habit.

Grinder blades wear out every six to eight weeks and the drift they cause
is slow enough to miss. When shots creep past 32 seconds for a whole
morning even after adjustment, the blades are done — order replacements
the same day, do not "wait for the weekend" because the courier does not
run Sundays.

Humidity changes the grind setting. On rainy days the beans absorb
moisture and the same setting pulls slower; on dry days, faster. Note
the weather at dial-in and expect to re-dial after any weather front
passes. The dial is not a set-and-forget number, it is a morning
instrument reading.

## Section 3: Closing and cash

The till is counted twice: once by the closing lead at 21:00 and once by
the opening lead at 6:05 the next morning. Both counts go in the cash
book with initials. A variance over 5 pounds triggers a same-week
count-together session with the manager — not as punishment, but because
variance that survives a week becomes untraceable.

Tips are split by hours worked, not by shift block. The point-of-sale
system exports the hours report at close; the closing lead divides the
jar by total hours and rounds up to the nearest pound per person. Cash
tips stay in the jar, card tips arrive in the Friday payroll run.

Waste is logged before it is thrown away. Every pour-out, expired
pastry, and dropped cup goes in the waste log with a reason code, because
the monthly P&L treats unlogged waste as theft-pattern noise and hides
the real problems, like over-ordering on Tuesdays.

## Section 4: Staffing and breaks

A shift needs a minimum of three people: one on till, one on bar, one
floating. Below three, the floater merges into bar and the till queue
grows past the door within twenty minutes — we measured this in October
2025 during the bridge closure week and the queue hit the door in
eighteen minutes flat. Call the on-call list before you hit three, not
after.

Breaks are 30 minutes paid, taken in order of who started first. No one
works more than four hours without a break; the rota software flags it,
but the flag only works if the rota was actually built in the software
and not on a sticky note. Breaks missed twice in a week to the same
person is a staffing failure, not a personal choice.

New baristas train for two weeks on bar before they touch the till,
because till mistakes cost money quietly while bar mistakes are loud and
fixable. The training checklist lives in the binder by the office door;
a trainee signs each stage, and the shift lead countersigns.

## Section 5: Health and incidents

Any injury, however small, goes in the incident book within the shift it
happened. The book is a legal document: date, time, person, what
happened, what was done, signature. Band-aids are in the first aid kit
by the grinder; blue-detectable plasters only, because a normal plaster
in a drink is a hygiene incident and a blue one is a near-miss.

Equipment failures are tagged out, not worked around. A broken steam
wand is not "the one we do hot water from" — it is off, tagged, and the
tag carries the date and the fault. Working around a fault teaches the
whole team to ignore tags, and the day someone ignores a tag on the
oven, that habit is the injury.

The emergency contact tree is on the office wall. In a real emergency
call 999 first, the tree second. The tree is for coverage and decisions,
not for permission to call for help.
