# X_dial_vs_blades — Is a slow shot a dial problem or a blade problem?

## Side 1: A slow shot is a dial problem

Humidity and bean moisture shift the pull time daily; the same setting
pulls slower on rainy days. The dial is a morning instrument reading, so
a slow morning means re-dial today, within the same grind hardware.

> On rainy days the beans absorb
"moisture and the same setting pulls slower; on dry days, faster." — sources/S_ops_handbook.md
## Side 2: A slow shot is a blade problem

When shots creep past 32 seconds for a whole morning even after
adjustment, the blades are worn out, and no setting fixes a mechanical
cause. The blade camp says: order replacements the same day.

> When shots creep past 32 seconds for a whole
morning even after adjustment, the blades are done — order replacements
the same day, do not "wait for the weekend" because the courier does not
"run Sundays." — sources/S_ops_handbook.md
## Discriminator

Time and persistence separate the two: a humidity drift corrects with a
same-morning adjustment and follows the weather; blade wear persists
past adjustment for a whole morning and worsens over weeks. The
discriminator is "even after adjustment" — if adjustment works, it is
the dial; if it does not, it is the blades.
